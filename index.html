THE SCIENCE

Why does a spacecraft tumble?

In a torque-free environment, angular momentum and rotational kinetic energy remain constant. Yet the direction of a spacecraft's spin relative to its body can change.

For a rigid body with three distinct principal moments of inertia, spinning about the smallest or largest principal axis is stable to small perturbations. Spinning about the intermediate axis is unstable: tiny disturbances can grow into dramatic flips.

Important: The spacecraft does not automatically settle onto a stable axis in ideal zero gravity. Damping or active attitude control is required to dissipate rotational energy and settle its motion.
